Who can write a review, and when?

A quick rundown of who's eligible to leave a Trustpilot review, the situations that don't qualify, and what your review needs to include to stay up.

🕑 2 min read · Updated Aug 2026

When can you write a review?

Our Guidelines for Reviewers say you can review a business if you're over 18 and you've had a genuine, recent experience with them. That includes:

  • You bought a product or service — e.g. you got a haircut at a salon, or bought a pair of jeans secondhand.
  • You browsed a website — even if you didn't finish the purchase. If you added items to a cart but left because the site was slow, you can still review that experience.
  • You spoke with the business — by phone, email, or live chat, even without buying anything.
💡
Tip — keep a record
Save something that shows you had the experience, like a receipt or a chat screenshot, in case your review is ever questioned later.

What else does your review need to meet?

On top of matching one of the situations above, make sure your review is:

  • Based only on your own experience — not something you heard about.
  • About something that happened within the past 12 months.

When can't you write a review?

A review won't stay up if it breaks one of the platform's core rules — most commonly, this one:

Rule — no incentivized reviews
You can't write a review if you were offered or received anything — a discount, cash, or loyalty points — in exchange for it. Businesses aren't allowed to offer incentives for reviews either.
